# TJR Human Systems Workbench V3
## Mission and Change Proposal
### Burnout-Aware Capacity, Regulation, Recovery and Redesign

**Status:** Proposed V3 uplift  
**Target:** Existing TJR Human Systems Workbench and supporting My Capacity Today data/services  
**Primary implementation audience:** Claude Code running on the TJR VM  
**Design stance:** Evidence-informed, neurodiversity-affirming, non-diagnostic, low cognitive load  
**Explicit exclusions:** diet-as-autism-treatment, detox, chelation, supplement protocols, vaccine/autism causation, autism “reversal”, suppression of harmless autistic regulation behaviours.

---

# 1. Mission

Evolve the Human Systems Workbench from a strong **daily capacity and regulation dashboard** into a **burnout-aware personal human systems operating model** that can:

1. show what state the system is in now;
2. recognise sustained strain rather than treating every difficult day as an isolated event;
3. make autistic burnout / burnout-like sustained depletion visible as a first-class longitudinal condition;
4. distinguish immediate regulation from genuine recovery;
5. identify environmental, sensory, social, executive, pain and compensation costs;
6. learn which supports actually help this individual in which contexts;
7. prevent temporary “good capacity” from being mistaken for recovery;
8. identify recurring conditions that should be redesigned rather than repeatedly coped with; and
9. support sustainable life participation rather than maximising productivity.

The central V3 question is:

> **What does my system need to recover, and what needs to change so I stop requiring this much recovery?**

The Workbench must remain a **management and reflection tool, not a diagnostic instrument**.

---

# 2. Why V3 Is Needed

The current Workbench has a strong foundation. It already presents:

- Capacity Today;
- System Posture;
- stimulation;
- pain;
- nervous-system state;
- executive function;
- masking / compensation;
- Capacity Balance;
- current drivers;
- “What My System Needs”;
- “My Next Move”;
- System Learning;
- What Helps Me;
- REVS position;
- Capacity & Recovery Conditions;
- Capacity Debt; and
- short trend views.

The current page correctly treats Capacity as an **outcome of interacting conditions rather than a collection of independent batteries**.

V3 should preserve that architecture.

The issue is that the current experience remains predominantly **today-oriented**.

A user can be GREEN / Sustainable today while simultaneously being in a much larger period of sustained depletion. Likewise, “ENGAGE” can be technically correct for the current check-in while being the wrong strategic posture if the wider system is recovering from prolonged overload.

V3 therefore introduces a second time horizon:

- **NOW:** What can my system tolerate today?
- **TRAJECTORY:** What condition has my system been operating in over days/weeks?

These must never be collapsed into one score.

# 3. V3 Product Principles

## 3.1 Capacity is not health

Capacity answers:

> What can I sustainably tolerate or do now?

It must not imply:

- healthy/unhealthy;
- autistic/not autistic;
- burned out/not burned out;
- productive/unproductive;
- successful/unsuccessful.

## 3.2 Burnout is not simply RED capacity

A RED day can occur without sustained burnout.

A GREEN morning can occur during a longer burnout recovery period.

V3 must model these separately.

## 3.3 Regulation is not the same as recovery

Regulation helps the system respond to its current state.

Recovery reduces accumulated strain and restores accessible function over time.

A person should not be repeatedly taught to regulate themselves sufficiently to tolerate an environment or demand that remains structurally unsustainable.

## 3.4 Do not optimise productivity

The system must optimise for:

> **sustainable life participation at an acceptable human cost**

not:

> maximum tasks completed while technically remaining functional.

## 3.5 Good days are not proof of recovery

Temporary capacity increases during sustained strain must not automatically trigger aggressive ENGAGE recommendations.

## 3.6 Natural regulation is valid

Harmless movement, repetition, fidgeting, pacing, reduced speech, withdrawal, focused interests, sensory seeking and sensory reduction may be regulatory.

Do not automatically classify them as symptoms to suppress.

## 3.7 Personal evidence and research evidence are different

The Workbench should eventually understand both:

- **general evidence:** whether a strategy is supported as a reasonable accommodation/regulation approach;
- **personal evidence:** whether it has actually helped this user in comparable states.

Neither should be presented as certainty.

# 4. V3 Core Human Systems Model

V3 should reason through seven connected layers.

## Layer 1 - STATE

What is happening now?

Examples:

- Capacity;
- stimulation;
- nervous-system activation;
- pain burden;
- executive accessibility;
- emotional load;
- social tolerance;
- body-signal clarity.

## Layer 2 - CONDITIONS

What conditions is the system operating inside?

Examples:

- sleep / recovery opportunity;
- physical state;
- sensory environment;
- social exposure;
- workload;
- interruption;
- predictability / uncertainty;
- transitions;
- location/environment;
- available recovery time.

Do not add diet, detox or biomedical autism-treatment variables.

## Layer 3 - LOAD

What is consuming capacity?

Include both visible and hidden load:

- work demand;
- decisions;
- pain;
- sensory processing;
- social processing;
- uncertainty;
- task switching;
- transition cost;
- executive effort;
- masking / compensation;
- recovery debt.

Conceptually:

`Actual Load = Visible Demand + Processing Cost + Compensation Cost + Transition Cost`

Do not present this as a medically validated equation or diagnostic score.

## Layer 4 - NATURAL REGULATION NEED

What does the system appear to want or need?

Examples:

- less input;
- more appropriate input;
- movement;
- repetition;
- quiet;
- reduced speech;
- solitude;
- connection;
- predictability;
- pressure / proprioceptive input;
- something familiar;
- something interesting;
- externalisation of thoughts;
- rest.

## Layer 5 - RESPONSE

What is the lowest-cost useful next action?

Candidate response families:

- REDUCE LOAD;
- REGULATE;
- RECOVER;
- PROTECT;
- ACCOMMODATE;
- RE-ENGAGE;
- REDESIGN.

## Layer 6 - LEARNING

What happened after the response?

At minimum:

- better;
- same;
- worse;
- not completed.

Learning should eventually incorporate context:

`State + Conditions + Load + Need + Response -> Outcome`

## Layer 7 - REDESIGN

What keeps recurring that should be changed instead of repeatedly endured?

Possible actions:

- remove;
- reduce;
- delegate;
- make predictable;
- accommodate;
- shorten;
- move;
- schedule recovery around;
- stop doing;
- change environment.

# 5. Elevate Autistic Burnout / Sustained System Strain

This is the highest-priority V3 change.

The Workbench must gain a persistent **Burnout & Recovery Trajectory** layer.

It must be able to represent:

`SUSTAINABLE -> ACCUMULATING STRAIN -> HIGH STRAIN -> BURNOUT-LIKE DEPLETION -> RECOVERING -> REBUILDING`

This is **not a diagnostic state machine**.

It is a personal management representation derived from longitudinal observations and/or explicit user self-identification.

## 5.1 Burnout identity/status

Support two independent fields:

### User framing

Examples:

- I identify this period as autistic burnout;
- I think I may be in burnout;
- I am recovering from burnout;
- I do not want a label;
- not set.

### System observation

Examples:

- insufficient longitudinal data;
- stable;
- accumulating strain;
- sustained high strain;
- burnout-like sustained depletion;
- recovery signals emerging;
- rebuilding.

The system must never silently convert its observation into a diagnosis.

Preferred language:

> “Your recent pattern shows sustained strain and increasing recovery demand.”

Avoid:

> “You have autistic burnout.”

unless merely displaying the user's own chosen framing.

---

# 6. Burnout Profile

Create a longitudinal Burnout Profile with five core domains.

## 6.1 Exhaustion

Track patterns such as:

- baseline energy;
- exhaustion not resolved by ordinary rest;
- recovery time after normal demand;
- number/frequency of depleted days;
- need for daytime withdrawal/rest;
- recovery requirement after work/social activity.

## 6.2 Functional Accessibility

Track whether normally available functions are becoming harder to access.

Examples:

- finding words;
- speaking;
- making decisions;
- task initiation;
- task switching;
- planning;
- leaving home;
- self-care;
- household tasks;
- work participation;
- social interaction;
- managing appointments;
- tolerating ordinary input.

Use **accessibility**, **difficulty** or **cost**, not “loss of ability” unless the user explicitly uses that language.

## 6.3 Tolerance

Track changes in tolerance for:

- sound;
- light;
- people;
- conversation;
- touch;
- interruption;
- uncertainty;
- transitions;
- decisions;
- pain;
- environmental complexity.

The key question is not only “is this difficult?” but:

> **Has my tolerance changed from my own baseline?**

## 6.4 Compensation Cost

Split the current Masking / Compensation field into:

- amount;
- type;
- felt necessity;
- cost afterward.

Possible types:

- suppressing movement;
- suppressing sensory needs;
- performing expected social behaviour;
- forcing speech;
- hiding pain/fatigue;
- forcing eye contact if relevant;
- hiding need for solitude;
- pretending to understand/process immediately;
- suppressing need for routine/predictability.

Do not assume all compensation is harmful.

## 6.5 Recovery Trajectory

Derive/display:

- improving;
- stable;
- volatile;
- deteriorating;
- insufficient data.

This should be based on longitudinal patterns, not one check-in.

# 7. Burnout-Aware System Posture

The existing `SYSTEM POSTURE = ENGAGE` is useful but too immediate when viewed alone.

Retain **Today's Posture**, but introduce a second strategic posture.

Example:

```text
TODAY'S CAPACITY
GREEN - Sustainable

TODAY'S POSTURE
STEADY

RECOVERY TRAJECTORY
BURNOUT RECOVERY / HIGH STRAIN

STRATEGIC POSTURE
PROTECT + RECOVER
```

A GREEN day during burnout recovery should be allowed to produce:

> “More capacity is available today. Recent recovery demand remains elevated. Use the extra margin selectively rather than treating today as evidence that recovery is complete.”

Potential strategic postures:

- ENGAGE;
- STEADY;
- PROTECT;
- RECOVER;
- STABILISE;
- RE-ENGAGE;
- REBUILD;
- REDESIGN.

# 8. Burnout Recovery Stages

Add a management pathway:

## 1. PROTECT

Objective:

> Stop adding avoidable depletion.

System behaviour:

- reduce optional load;
- minimise unnecessary decisions;
- protect sensory tolerance;
- reduce forced social performance;
- favour low-cost interventions;
- surface immediate accommodations.

## 2. STABILISE

Objective:

> Find conditions in which the system stops oscillating so severely.

Focus:

- predictability;
- lower cognitive demand;
- reliable basic routines;
- sensory protection;
- sufficient recovery windows;
- fewer concurrent changes.

## 3. RECOVER

Objective:

> Create actual replenishment rather than merely sufficient regulation to continue.

Support multiple recovery modes:

### Passive
- quiet;
- sleep/rest;
- darkness;
- reduced demand.

### Active
- movement;
- walking;
- shower;
- music;
- pet interaction;
- low-demand familiar activities.

### Immersive
- focused interests;
- reading;
- building/making;
- gaming;
- research;
- creative work.

These are candidate categories, not universal prescriptions.

## 4. RE-ENGAGE

Objective:

> Carefully reintroduce meaningful participation.

Rules:

- prioritise personally meaningful activity;
- avoid automatically filling newly available capacity;
- watch post-activity recovery cost;
- learn sustainable dose.

## 5. REBUILD

Objective:

> Gradually increase usable capacity only when recovery is holding.

Rebuild must be gated.

Do not recommend capacity expansion simply because one or two good check-ins occurred.

## 6. REDESIGN

Objective:

> Change recurring conditions that repeatedly create unsustainable cost.

# 9. REVS V3

The current Workbench displays:

- RECOGNISE - Ongoing
- REGULATE - Current priority
- REBUILD - Not yet
- REDESIGN - As patterns emerge

V3 should evolve this to:

> **RECOGNISE -> REGULATE -> RECOVER -> REBUILD -> REDESIGN**

These are not completion stages.

They are management orientations that can overlap.

During burnout, expected behaviour may be:

```text
RECOGNISE   Ongoing
REGULATE    Supportive
RECOVER     Current priority
REBUILD     Gated
REDESIGN    Active where recurring strain is clear
```

The UI should explicitly retain the concept that this is a **management orientation, not a completion score**.

# 10. Sensory Regulation Upgrade

Replace the single high-level stimulation concept as the only sensory representation.

Keep the quick summary:

- Too much;
- Balanced;
- Not enough.

Add an optional deeper Sensory Profile.

Potential channels:

- auditory;
- visual;
- touch;
- smell;
- movement / vestibular;
- pressure / proprioceptive;
- temperature;
- environmental complexity.

For each relevant channel allow:

- reduce/avoid;
- neutral;
- seek/helpful;
- context dependent;
- unknown.

Also learn:

- environment;
- intensity;
- controllability;
- cumulative exposure;
- what helped.

The Workbench should be able to say:

> “Overall stimulation is balanced, but auditory load is high.”

This is more useful than collapsing all sensory experience into one value.

# 11. Natural Regulation Response

Add a low-friction question/path:

> **What does my system seem to want right now?**

Candidate options:

- less input;
- more input;
- move;
- fidget/repeat;
- quiet;
- stop talking;
- be alone;
- connect with someone safe;
- something familiar;
- something interesting;
- pressure / sensory comfort;
- get thoughts out;
- rest;
- I don't know.

Also support:

> **Am I stopping myself from doing something that may help because it feels inappropriate, inconvenient or noticeable?**

This should feed compensation-cost learning, not shame or correction.

# 12. Predictability as a Regulation Variable

Add **Predictability / Change Load** to conditions.

Simple scale:

- clear / predictable;
- some uncertainty;
- lots changing;
- I do not know what happens next.

Potential interventions:

- define the next step;
- reduce open decisions;
- clarify what will happen;
- set an exit time;
- create a simple sequence;
- keep one part of the day familiar.

This should not become a rigid-routine score.

# 13. Body Signal Clarity

Do not assume impaired interoception.

Add a neutral individual field:

> **How clear are my body's signals right now?**

- clear;
- somewhat clear;
- hard to interpret;
- no idea.

This can help explain why the user may need external prompts or structured options without assigning a diagnostic explanation.

# 14. Capacity Debt -> Burnout Load

Retain **Capacity Debt**.

Definition:

> Did maintaining today's output appear to increase tomorrow's recovery requirement?

Add a separate longitudinal construct:

## Burnout Load / Sustained Strain

This should not be a simplistic medical score.

Derive a cautious trend from combinations of:

- repeated depleted/stretched capacity;
- elevated recovery time;
- reduced functional accessibility;
- falling sensory/social tolerance;
- high compensation cost;
- persistent load;
- inadequate recovery opportunity;
- volatility;
- capacity debt.

Preferred UI language:

> “Sustained strain is elevated.”

or:

> “Recovery requirements have remained high while tolerance has reduced.”

Avoid false precision such as:

> “Burnout score: 83%.”

# 15. Personal Experiment Engine

Upgrade `WORTH TESTING` from narrative copy into a real object.

Schema concept:

```yaml
experiment:
  id:
  hypothesis:
  target_condition:
  proposed_change:
  baseline_window:
  trial_window:
  outcome_measures:
  status:
  result:
  confidence:
  notes:
```

Example:

```text
WORTH TESTING

Hypothesis:
Using a quieter work location before afternoon overload may reduce
post-work recovery cost.

Baseline:
4 of 5 office afternoons reached Stretched or Depleted.

Trial:
Use quiet-room work before overload for two weeks.

Observe:
- 3pm capacity
- sensory load
- post-work recovery requirement
- next-morning capacity
```

Experiments must:

- test one meaningful change where possible;
- avoid creating excessive change load;
- be reversible where practical;
- not masquerade as medical treatment;
- be stoppable if worse.

# 19. System Learning Upgrade

Retain the existing:

- WHAT I KNOW;
- POSSIBLE PATTERN;
- WORTH TESTING.

Improve it to:

## WHAT I KNOW

Descriptive observations only.

## POSSIBLE PATTERN

Cautious inference.

Always include data sufficiency.

## WORTH TESTING

Actionable experiment object.

## WHAT CHANGED

After an experiment has enough observations, show whether the hypothesis appears supported personally.

Do not allow LLM-generated pattern language to outrun the underlying data.

# 20. What Helps Me -> What Helps, When

Current `What Helps Me` should become context-sensitive.

Instead of:

> Change posture helped 5/7 times.

Prefer:

> Change posture  
> Better 5/7 overall  
> Most helpful when: restless / understimulated  
> Limited evidence when: depleted  
> Last tried: today

The purpose is not to build a universal ranking.

It is to learn:

> **What helps me, in which state, under which conditions?**

# 21. Things I Should Change, Not Keep Coping With

Make this a first-class V3 section.

A candidate becomes eligible when there is repeated evidence that:

- the same condition drives strain;
- regulation repeatedly treats the consequence;
- recovery cost remains high;
- an accommodation or structural change is plausible.

Example:

```text
REDESIGN CANDIDATE

Office auditory load

Observed:
High auditory load appears in 7 of 9 stretched/depleted office check-ins.

Current coping:
Headphones + leaving the floor after overload.

Possible redesign:
Use quieter work location before overload.

Status:
Worth testing
```

This section should connect directly to Personal Experiments.

# 22. Burnout-Aware Recommendation Rules

Implement guardrails before adding sophisticated prediction.

## Rule A

If today's capacity improves but sustained strain remains high:

- do not automatically move to aggressive ENGAGE;
- prefer STEADY / PROTECT;
- explicitly warn against interpreting one good period as complete recovery.

## Rule B

If repeated regulation is required for the same driver:

- increase redesign priority.

## Rule C

If functional accessibility is worsening despite apparently acceptable capacity:

- elevate sustained-strain concern.

## Rule D

If recovery time is increasing:

- reduce recommended load before recommending capacity expansion.

## Rule E

If compensation cost is high:

- consider accommodation/reduction before asking for additional coping.

## Rule F

If data is insufficient:

- say `insufficient data`;
- do not fabricate a trajectory.

## Rule G

When burnout strain is high:

- interventions must be low cognitive load;
- default to one next action;
- avoid long lists.

# 31. Acceptance Criteria

V3 is not complete until the following scenarios work.

## Scenario 1 - Green day during burnout

Given:

- today's capacity = GREEN;
- recent recovery demand = high;
- sustained strain = elevated;
- user identifies as recovering from autistic burnout;

Then:

- UI must not imply the overall system is fully recovered;
- strategic posture should remain protective/recovery-oriented;
- next action should not encourage filling all available capacity.

## Scenario 2 - Repeated sensory overload

Given:

- multiple office check-ins show high auditory load;
- headphones help after overload;
- recovery cost remains high;

Then:

- system should identify a possible recurring pattern;
- create/offer a redesign experiment before simply recommending headphones again.

## Scenario 3 - Function getting harder

Given:

- capacity scores are relatively stable;
- task initiation, word finding and decision making are becoming harder;
- recovery time is rising;

Then:

- system should elevate sustained-strain concern;
- it must not conclude that stable capacity means stable recovery.

## Scenario 4 - Natural regulation

Given:

- user reports needing movement/repetition;
- movement has previously helped;

Then:

- system may suggest movement as regulation;
- it must not frame harmless movement as behaviour to eliminate.

## Scenario 5 - Insufficient data

Given:

- only one or two relevant observations;

Then:

- system states that evidence is insufficient;
- no confident burnout trajectory or causal claim is generated.

## Scenario 6 - Personal intervention evidence

Given:

- a strategy works in restless/understimulated states;
- the same strategy does not help depleted states;

Then:

- `What Helps Me` must show context rather than a misleading universal success score.

# 32. Testing Requirements

Add tests for:

- today-state vs longitudinal-state separation;
- burnout user framing vs system inference;
- insufficient-data handling;
- REVS RECOVER stage;
- burnout-aware posture guardrails;
- functional-accessibility trend;
- compensation-cost trend;
- sensory-channel capture;
- natural-regulation responses;
- experiment lifecycle;
- redesign-candidate promotion;
- evidence metadata;
- legacy records with missing V3 fields;
- mobile rendering;
- null / unknown values.

Existing V01/V02 data must remain readable.

# 33. Non-Goals for Initial V3

Do not block V3 on:

- machine-learning prediction;
- clinical diagnostic scoring;
- wearable integrations;
- complex medical integrations;
- automated workplace-adjustment generation;
- external clinician portals;
- population benchmarking.

V3 should first become **correct, useful and personally adaptive** using the data already being generated.

# 35. V3 North Star

The Human Systems Workbench should ultimately be able to answer six questions quickly:

1. **What state am I in today?**
2. **What is costing my system the most?**
3. **Am I dealing with a momentary state or sustained strain?**
4. **What does my system need next?**
5. **What has actually helped me in similar conditions?**
6. **What should I change instead of continuing to cope with?**

The product succeeds when it becomes less necessary to repeatedly ask:

> “How do I push through this?”

and increasingly possible to ask:

> **“How do I build conditions in which my system can participate sustainably?”**

---

# 36. Final Product Position

**TJR Human Systems Workbench V3** is a personal, evidence-informed, non-diagnostic operating view of:

> **capacity + regulation + autistic-burnout-aware recovery + environmental fit + personal learning + redesign**

It does not attempt to cure autism, optimise normality or maximise output.

Its purpose is to help the user **recognise their system, reduce unnecessary cost, recover from sustained overload, learn what genuinely helps, and redesign recurring conditions that are not sustainable.**
